JURISDICTION: New York
COURT: Supreme Court, New York County
YEAR: 1989
JUDGE: Gangel-Jacob

Golub v. Golub, 139 Misc.2d 440 (Sup. Ct. N.Y. County 1989)

The parties were married in 1973. During the marriage, the wife pursued a career as an actress and model, achieving substantial commercial success and public recognition. The husband contributed to her career in various ways throughout the marriage, including providing financial support during lean periods, managing household affairs, and offering personal and professional encouragement that enabled her to pursue demanding professional opportunities.

The wife argues that her career and celebrity status are not marital property because they are personal to her, are not licensed or transferable, and fluctuate in value depending on public taste and commercial opportunity. She contends that there is no reliable method of valuing such an asset and that subjecting it to equitable distribution would effectively give the husband a claim on her future earnings.

The court disagrees. The skills of an artisan, actor, professional athlete, or any person whose expertise has enabled that person to become an exceptional wage earner should be valued as marital property subject to equitable distribution. It is the enhanced earning capacity that a career generates that is the distributable asset, not the career itself as an abstract concept.

The court finds that the husband's contributions to the wife's professional development during the marriage were substantial and direct. He provided financial stability that allowed her to accept roles without regard to immediate compensation, managed domestic responsibilities so she could focus on her work, and offered consistent support that contributed materially to the development of her career and earning capacity during the marriage.

To the extent the appreciation in the wife's career and earning capacity during the marriage was due in part to the husband's contributions, that appreciation is marital property subject to equitable distribution. The court orders a hearing to determine the appropriate method of valuation and the equitable share to be awarded to the husband.
